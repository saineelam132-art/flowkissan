* Calibrated on the first 21 days (January) only; scored on the next 288.6 days.
* **False flags: 15 in 288.6 days = 1.56 per 30 days** (target ≈ 1). Readings flagged: 2.9 %.
* By layer: daily 5, instant 5, slow (CUSUM) 5. By residual: R10 5, R1 5, R5 4, R7T 1.
* Readings with φ_inst ≥ 3: 0.68 % (a perfect Gaussian model would give 0.13 %): honest residuals have heavier tails than the model assumes, which is why a flag needs 3 readings in a row.
* Glycol watermark: 0 of 286 honest days below the flag line (z median 10.5, 1st percentile 6.0).

Why not the old memory `S = S + Φ − 1` with Φ = max|z|: on this honest record max|z| averages 1.63, so S grows by 181 per day and reaches 6061 after 30 days – it alarms forever. The CUSUM used here subtracts the expected χ² (m) plus κ, so its honest drift is negative.

## Limits (plain English)

* A fully consistent fake – every owner sensor taken from a good model of the same room on the real weather – breaks no physics: **φ alone cannot catch it.** Only data the owner cannot make (the weather service, the inverter log, the utility meter, and above all the secret watermark) can. That is why the watermark and external references are part of the design, not extras.
* The watermark proves the GLYCOL readings are live. It cannot prove the room probes are honest if the owner fakes only those; then the physics residuals (R1, R9, R2) have to catch it.
* Slow lies smaller than the season-to-season model error can be learnt into the baseline over weeks (14-day memory, bounded to 4 σ).
* All of this is tested on a simulated plant. Real sensors, a real room and a real attacker will differ – see 'real-world data still needed'.
